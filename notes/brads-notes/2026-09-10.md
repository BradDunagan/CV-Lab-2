# 2026-09-10

# The offset sweep: it is slant

The question 09-05 left open. 282 of 385 invented helmet segments classified
`occlusion`, sitting on ~9 cm depth steps indistinguishable from the ones that
matched, and two candidates: the matcher losing a fitted segment among
thousands of tiny truth edges, or `explain` reading grazing slant as a step.

`explain` already has the knob — `offset`, default 2.5 px, the lateral distance
either side of a detection at which it samples the passes. Six values, 1.0 /
1.5 / 2.5 / 4 / 6 / 8, over `generated/helmet-256`, `helmet-512` and `clutter`.
No re-render: 18 runs, about three seconds each, on the images already on disk.
`cube1` has no AOV passes, so the flat control had to come from the clutter
scene's table instead.

The default-offset run reproduces 09-05 exactly — 983 / 598 / 385, occlusion
282, shading 46, texture 32, crease 25 — so this is the same apparatus.

## The share tracks the offset, everywhere

Invented segments:

```
offset    helmet-256                          clutter
          occl  shading texture               occl  shading
1.0        57%    23%     16%                  0%    100%
1.5        63%    18%     14%                  3%     97%
2.5        73%    12%      8%                  8%     90%
4          83%     9%      6%                 10%     89%
6          90%     4%      4%                 11%     76%
8          92%     3%      3%                 19%     64%
```

Detections, matches and misses are identical in every row — `offset` reaches
only `explain`, as it should.

**The internal control fails.** Matched segments drift as hard as invented
ones: helmet-256 `hit`/occlusion goes 65% → 99% across the same range. So this
is not something happening to unmatched detections; the classifier is
offset-dependent on everything.

And the clutter scene at 1.0 px says 100% shading — which is exactly as
untrustworthy as 73% occlusion, in the other direction. Picking the offset that
gives the answer you like is available here, which is the reason not to.

## The test that settles it

A genuine depth step is the size of the step: sample further either side and
you measure the same difference. A slant is a gradient, so what you measure
scales with how far you sampled. Ratio of each detection's `depthStep` at
offset 8 to its value at 1.0 — the sampling distance differing by exactly 8×:

```
subject      group at default offset    n     <2x    >=4x   median ratio
helmet-256   invented / occlusion      282     13     241      8.03
helmet-512   invented / occlusion      974     34     911      8.01
clutter      MATCHED  / occlusion       61     52       8      1.12
helmet-256   MATCHED  / occlusion      491    199     240      3.67
```

8.00 against an 8× change in sampling distance, to two figures, over hundreds
of features: a constant depth gradient. The clutter scene's matched occlusions
— real silhouettes against a background — come back at **1.12**, which is what
a real step looks like and rules out the offset-1.0 reading being suppressed
for everything.

**241 of 282 are grazing slant. Thirteen survive.**

## What that costs the previous three weeks of numbers

- The T-junction story on the helmet was 282 segments and is 13. The clutter
  scene's 37% invented corners have not had this test run on them and cannot be
  trusted until they do.
- `explain` misclassifies **matched** detections at the same rate — 240 of 491
  helmet occlusions are slant — so every AOV breakdown on record carries this,
  not just the invented column. That includes the 08-27 and 09-05 tallies, the
  users' manual, the README and driver.js.
- What survives untouched: the shading finding on the clutter scene. Those 111
  invented segments are classified by the *absence* of a depth step, and the
  two confirmed by eye — the specular highlight on the visor, the contact
  shadow on the floor — are unaffected by how far the depth was sampled.

## The fix is not a smaller offset

An absolute 2 cm threshold read at a fixed pixel distance is the defect. What a
benign slant produces scales with the offset, with resolution and with camera
distance, so no constant is right. The test would have to predict the step that
slant alone accounts for — from the normal pass and the view direction — and
call it an occlusion only on the excess.

Not attempted here. `explain` is version 1 and changing its classification
changes every number in this repository that came out of it, which is a thing
to do deliberately and with the overlay open.

## What is still inferred rather than measured

Slant itself. Linear-in-offset is slant's signature and nothing else here
produces it, but I did not read the normal pass and compute normal · view. That
is the way to still be wrong about the mechanism while being right that the
depth test is offset-dependent — and it is one more pass over data already on
disk.

## Left on disk

Nothing in the repository. The 18 result directories (732 MB), the six
`explained-<offset>.lab` variants and the two analysis scripts were written to
a session scratchpad, which is disposable. Reproducing it is `sed` on
`pipelines/explained.lab` to set `offset=` on the two `explain` lines, then the
same `npm run lab` invocation 09-05 used, once per value.

# Slant, measured

The thing the sweep left inferred. Read the normal pass at the same sample
points `explain` uses, and ask a sharper question than "is the slant high":
**does one tangent plane through the midpoint account for the depth difference
`explain` measured?** Predicted from the plane, over measured, per sample pair.
A continuous surface gives 1. A real step gives ~0, because a plane fitted at
one side cannot reach across a discontinuity.

PNG decode in plain node — the AOVs are 8-bit RGBA, non-interlaced, and a
40-line inflate-and-unfilter reads them without needing Chromium or the addon.
Depth unpacked with the same expression `explain` uses, from the `.gt.json`'s
`maxDepth`. Camera from the same file: position, target, `fov` 50, size 256.

## The normal pass is in VIEW space, not world

Not documented anywhere, and the fit says so twice over.

```
convention                          plane/measured over the 241:  p25   p50   p75
normals rotated world -> camera                                  0.72  1.16  1.28
normals used as they are (view space)                            0.93  0.99  1.00
```

Corroborated independently: the normal at the same pixel over three cameras at
0/+2.94/-2.94 in x reads `(-0.40,-0.26,0.88)`, `(0.10,-0.43,0.90)`,
`(-0.40,-0.74,0.54)` — dominated by +Z in all three. World-space normals of a
convex object seen from three sides could not do that; view-space ones, where
+Z is "facing the camera", must.

## The result

helmet-256, offset 2.5, view-space normals:

```
group                              n     slant p10/med/p90     plane/measured p25 p50 p75
invented / occlusion / slant     241     38.9  64.0  69.5        0.93  0.99  1.00
invented / occlusion / step       13     51.5  64.8  73.9        0.12  0.55  1.00
matched  / occlusion / step      199     43.7  64.1  73.7        0.02  0.07  0.18
```

and the clutter scene, which has a flat table to be graze-viewed:

```
invented / occlusion / slant       9     75.7  76.0  76.8        1.01  1.01  1.01
invented / shading   / slant     111     49.8  56.0  59.6        0.99  1.00  1.01
matched  / occlusion / step       52     49.4  67.2  73.9        0.01  0.02  0.06
```

**0.99 against 0.07.** The depth difference under those 241 detections is what a
continuous surface at the measured slant produces, to within 1%, and the 199
real ones are not: a tangent plane accounts for 7% of their step. The mechanism
is confirmed and it is not inference any more.

## The part that changes how to fix it

**Slant does not discriminate.** Median slant is 64.0° under the slant group
and 64.1° under the genuine steps — the same, because both live near a
silhouette, which is exactly where a surface turns away and also where one
surface ends in front of another. A threshold on slant would be no better than
the threshold on depth it replaced.

What discriminates is the *residual*: measured minus what the local plane
predicts. That quantity is already available from data `explain` reads today —
it needs the normal at the midpoint, the depth there, and the camera, which is
the one thing it does not currently have. `maxDepth` comes from the `.gt.json`;
`camera` is in the same file and is not read.

So the shape of the fix is: pass the camera through, predict the step from the
tangent plane, and test the excess. Still not attempted — it changes every
number this repository has published from `explain`.

## Caveats

- The 13 "real step" invented segments sit at 0.12–1.00, which is a mixed
  population and too few to say more about. They are the only remaining
  candidates for the T-junction story on this subject.
- One scene family, one detector configuration, default `explain` thresholds.
- The plane prediction uses the normal at the midpoint of the pair, which on a
  genuinely curved surface is itself an approximation — biased toward
  under-predicting, which would make 0.99 a slight underestimate rather than an
  overestimate.
