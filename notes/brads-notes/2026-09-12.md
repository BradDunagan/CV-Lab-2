# 2026-09-12

# The 88, taken apart

The first open item in the helmet review (09-11 §12): **88 invented segments
that `explain` v2 still calls `occlusion`**. A continuous surface does not
account for their depth difference and no truth edge pairs with them. Three
candidates were on the list: T-junctions, segments `match` failed to pair, and
the tangent-plane prediction going wrong on a curved patch. Nothing separated
them.

(The 09-11 T-junction section says 60. That was one row of 09-10's
cross-tabulation, the v1 slant group. The whole bucket is 88. Same run.)

Everything below is helmet-256, the six views on disk, `explain` v2 at its
defaults. A plain-node loader reads the `.features.json`, `.gt.json` and AOV
PNGs and reproduces **all 983 stored `explain` records exactly** (0 cause
mismatches, 0.0 difference in `depthExcess`), so this is the same apparatus.

## The answer, first

```
the 88                                         n   what it is
no depth step: curvature                      37   the plane extrapolated over a curved surface
real step, 2-2.5 px BESIDE the detection      12   explain's sample reaching the next contour
small lip, 2-5 cm                             16   bolt rings, panel edges; step vs fold unresolved
real step UNDER the detection                 23   by eye: 13 junction of two contours
                                                            4 cuts a corner of one contour
                                                            3 along a contour, match's angle test
                                                            3 not a step under it after all
```

**Curvature is the largest share, T-junction-like junctions are about one in
six, and a clean `match` failure is three.** Two mechanisms that were not on
the list account for 28 more.

The 88 are not 88 places. Twelve are the same detection under both light
levels, so it is **76 distinct places**. Eleven of the twelve pairs land in the
same class; p0-l0 #40 / p0-l1 #48 do not.

## Dead end 1: the truth set cannot arbitrate here

First reading of the match records: 65 of the 88 lie within 3 px of a visible
truth edge (median **1.1 px**) and fail only on angle (median 28°). The
foot points of the per-sample nearest truth run within ~15° of the detection
for about 45 of them. That looked like `match` comparing against one 2-4 px
facet of a zigzag polyline.

**The control killed it.** The same measure on each segment rotated 90° about
its midpoint:

```
                          runs along truth    rotated copy does too    within 1.5 px, median (rotated)
matched occlusion   453         410                  120                    1.00 (0.67)
the 88               88          32                   21                    0.63 (0.67)
```

The 88 sit in patches so dense with mesh edges that a perpendicular segment is
as close to geometry as the real one. Proximity to truth means nothing there.

**Dead end 1b.** A matcher that filters candidates by angle first and then
measures distance rescues 45 of the 88. It also takes rotated-copy hits from
37 to 127 across all 983, and 27 of those 45 would match turned sideways. Too
permissive to be evidence of anything.

What does survive the control is matching against **occluding truth only**
(silhouette + boundary), which is much sparser: matched occlusions hit 376 of
453 and their rotated copies 19. Against it the 88 mostly still fail (next
sections).

## So ask the depth pass, not the truth

At each of `explain`'s seven crossings, sample depth densely across the line
(0.25 px, ±4.5 px) and fit two lines that break at a searched point `u0`
(within ±2.5 px), with a free jump `h` at the break. Samples within 0.75 px of
`u0` are left out, because bilinear sampling ramps any step. A fold fits with
`h ≈ 0`, since the two lines meet; a step fits with `h` = the step; a gentle
curve fits with a small `h`. Per segment, the median `h` over the crossings.

Calibrated against the populations whose answer is known:

```
jump h (m)                 n     p25    p50    p75    p90
invented shading         218   0.000  0.000  0.000  0.002
matched  crease           47   0.004  0.011  0.027  0.042
matched  texture          82   0.006  0.010  0.020  0.028
matched  occlusion       453   0.036  0.205  2.394  3.258
the 88                    88   0.009  0.030  0.147  0.782
```

**The 88's median is 3 cm, against `explain`'s 2 cm threshold.** At 256 px the
helmet is ~3.5 m away with f = 274 px, so one pixel covers ~1.3 cm of surface.
A 2 cm residual is about what a fold or a curve produces over explain's 5 px
reach, with no step at all.

**Dead end 2**, recorded so it is not repeated: an earlier version took the
largest line-to-line gap over positions near the line. Matched creases read
2.4 cm on it, because away from the fold one window straddles the kink.
A later version widened the break search to ±4 px with windows that moved with
it; matched occlusions fell to a 9 mm median, because the lowest-residual
break was one whose windows missed the step entirely. The version above keeps
one fixed window for every break position.

Tiers: `h < 2 cm` no step · `2-5 cm` marginal · `≥ 5 cm` a step, which is
**under** the detection if the break sits within 2 px of it and **beside** it
if it is pinned at 2-2.5 px.

## The confirmation: how the residual scales with explain's own offset

The same kind of test 09-10 used, and it separates the mechanisms by exponent.
Across a step the residual does not depend on how far out you look (exponent
0). A plane extrapolated across a fold misses by slope × distance (1). A plane
extrapolated over curvature misses by curvature × distance² (2).

```
                          n   excess @1.25   @2.5    @5    exponent 1.25→2.5  2.5→5   still ≥2 cm @1.25
matched occlusion       453       0.074     0.133  0.318         0.41          0.17        341
matched crease           47       0.003     0.010  0.039         1.54          2.15          0
the 88: step under       23       0.159     0.306  0.377         0.43          0.21         19
the 88: step beside      12       0.015     0.065  1.217         2.01          2.31          4
the 88: marginal         16       0.027     0.041  0.054         0.90          0.43         10
the 88: no step          37       0.009     0.028  0.143         1.94          2.08          1
```

**No step: exponent 1.94 → 2.08.** Quadratic, which is curvature, reached by
an independent route from the jump fit. 36 of 37 fall under 2 cm at 1.25 px.

**Step under: 0.43 → 0.21**, the same signature as the matched occlusions. Those
steps are real.

**Step beside**: 1.5 cm at 1.25 px, 1.2 m at 5 px. Nothing under the line; the
sample reaches something next to it. The exponent here is a reach effect, not
a surface shape.

## What the overlay crops show

Contact sheets for all 88, three panels each: the picture with occluding truth
(visible cyan, hidden magenta), crease truth and explain's sample points; the
normal pass; a depth-step map.

- **No step (37).** Detections on the smooth crown and the rounded side parts.
  The normal pass is a smooth gradient under them with no break. They are
  reflections and shading on a curved surface seen at 60-70° slant (median
  normal step 12°), most of them 3.5-7 px inside a silhouette. These are what
  v2's tangent plane cannot handle.
- **Step beside (12).** Ten are the same picture: the lower edge of a panel, or
  a bolt ring's rim, running 2-3 px from a silhouette. The detection is the
  panel's edge, and explain's outer sample lands across the silhouette next to
  it. That is exactly the failure the `DEFAULTS` comment in `explain.js` warns
  about ("a wider reach starts reporting the NEXT feature along"). The other
  two sit at junctions.
- **Step under (23).** By eye:
  - **13 at a junction of two occluding contours**, where the hose meets the
    helmet body, where the chin cap meets the chin piece, where a bar runs
    into the outline, and where a ridge crosses a contour. The fitted segment
    is short (median 7 px) and runs between the two contours, more than 20°
    from each. These are the T-junction candidates. Most carry 20 cm-1 m of
    step, so the two surfaces are well apart in depth, which is what a
    projective junction looks like rather than parts touching. That is an
    inference from the jump size, not a check.
  - **4 cut the corner of a single contour** where the silhouette meets the
    floor. The truth has a vertex there (21 of the 23 have one within 3 px,
    against a 70% base rate on matched occlusions, so that alone is weak).
  - **3 lie along a visible silhouette or lip and fail `match` on angle**:
    p2-l1 #107 (0.3 px, 23°), p0-l1 #40, p2-l0 #29. These are the `match`
    failures, and they are near misses on the 20° limit rather than a pairing
    defect.
  - **3 are not a step under the line after all.** p1-l0 #26 and p1-l1 #32 (the
    same place) are a shading diagonal crossing a raised panel's lip.
    p2-l0 #143 is a floor shadow whose end touches the chin's contour. The
    jump fit found a step at part of their crossings and the median carried it.
- **Marginal (16).** Mostly the small raised bolt rings and panel edges, where
  a truth crease or occluder is 0.2-0.8 px away at 22-52°, since a ring a few
  px across is a polyline of a few facets and a 5-7 px detection is a chord
  across it. A 2-5 cm lip is below what this instrument separates from a fold
  at 256 px (matched creases reach 4.2 cm at p90), so these are left unsplit.

The by-eye counts are one reading of 7× crops. p2-l0 #143 and its other-light
twin p2-l1 #158 went into different sub-classes, and that is the size of the
uncertainty.

## What this says about the three candidates

1. **Plane extrapolation on curvature: 37**, and confirmed twice by independent
   routes, not inferred. It is the single largest share. v2 fixed slant
   (first order) and left curvature (second order), and at 256 px curvature over
   5 px crosses 2 cm routinely near a silhouette.
2. **T-junction-like junctions: ~13-15**, the only candidate the crops show
   directly. Whether each one is projective (a T) or parts meeting in 3D is not
   settled.
3. **`match` failing to pair: 3.** A clear failure, and each one is a near miss
   on the angle limit.

Plus two mechanisms nobody had listed: **explain reaching a neighbouring
contour (10)** and **lips at the resolution floor (16)**.

## What not to conclude from it

**A smaller offset is not the fix.** At 1.25 px the curvature group almost
vanishes, but 34 of the 321 matched occlusions with a ≥5 cm step drop below
2 cm too (112 of 453 overall). The exponent is what discriminates, not the
offset. A v3 that measures the residual at two offsets and rejects one growing
quadratically would target the 37 directly. Not attempted: it would change
every number `explain` has published, and it needs the same fixture-first
testing v2 got.

## Caveats

- One subject, one resolution, one detector configuration, default thresholds.
- The jump fit's break search is limited to ±2.5 px, explain's reach. A step
  beyond that shows as pinned at the limit, which is how "beside" was found;
  it cannot say how far beyond.
- Normals are 8-bit, so the plane predictions carry ~1/127 per component of
  quantisation. Not separately measured here.
- The junction class is by eye. The automated proxies (contours running in two
  directions nearby, a truth vertex nearby, membership of a detected corner)
  all have base rates of 60-100% on the matched segments, so none of them
  counts it.
