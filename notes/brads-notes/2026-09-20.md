# 2026-09-20

# Curved edges: what a probe on the nut says before any of it gets built

A design question came up — could the lab carry a `curved-edge` feature beside
`edge-segment`, given the nut is mostly curves? The architecture says yes
cheaply: `addon_kernels.c` already namespaces the type `edge-segment` so that
`edge-` leaves room for more, `match.js` dispatches on the detected type, and
`overlay-features.mjs` is a table keyed by type with an `unknown` fallback.

Rather than start on a conic fitter in C, one throwaway probe in JS over the
existing label map, to answer the only question that decides the shape of the
work: **on a real nut render, do the bore and chamfer survive `nms` +
`segments` + `merge` as coherent regions, or do they fragment?**

It decodes the PNG itself (the repo ships an encoder only), runs the real
kernels through the addon under plain Node, reads the i32 label map back with
`bufferRead`, and fits three models per label: a port of `cv_tls_line`, a
centred Kåsa circle, and an algebraic conic with a Sampson residual.

Scripts and overlays are in [`2026-09-20-arcs/`](2026-09-20-arcs/README.md).
The probe is not an implementation and its README says where a real fitter
would differ.

`assets/nut-1-10x-256x256.png`, 256×256. `gaussian sigma=1.4`, then `segments`
and `merge` at their defaults.

## The answer, first

**They fragment, and the fragmentation is the predicates doing exactly what
they were told.** Nothing is missing from the image.

```
74 labels after segments+merge, 1616 edge pixels
  72 stay straight, 2 prefer an arc     (51 of 1616 pixels)
```

Two. On a subject that is mostly curves, and with the rim, the threads and
both shadow boundaries all visibly traced in the overlay. A single `segments`
region is **too short to be curved**: it terminates before accumulating enough
sweep for a circle to beat a line.

Gates for "prefers an arc" were RMS gain ≥ 1.5, sweep ≥ 8°, radius ≤ the image
diagonal. They earn their keep — a circle is a strictly richer model than a
line, so without a margin gate the straight labels flip too. Three of the twelve
largest straight labels already have a gain between 1.07 and 1.21 on noise
alone.

So the work is **a chaining operation, not a rewrite of the growing
predicate**. `merge`'s architecture with one substitution — collinearity
replaced by "does one *circle* hold every pixel of both within maxResidual" —
recovers them:

```
28 joins accepted, 17 refused by the circle test
23 chains of 2+ labels; largest 83 px over 77.5° of sweep
```

Also worth recording: at `minMag=0.005` **`merge` joined nothing at all** —
74 labels in, 74 out. Expected in hindsight (pieces of a curve are not
collinear) but it means `merge` is inert on a curved subject, and a chain op
would not be duplicating it.

## Why they fragment: two ceilings, and the wrong one was assumed

An arc of chord `L` on radius `r` departs from its own TLS line by `L²/(12r)`,
so `maxResidual` caps `L` at `√(12·r·maxRes)`. Separately the gradient rotates
with the arc, so `angleTol` caps arc length at `r·angleTol`. `segments`
applies both greedily against the fit so far, so pieces should land just under
the smaller.

**`angleTol` binds in 9 of the 12 chains, not `maxResidual`.** I had assumed
the residual was the constraint; it only is above r ≈ 90 at these defaults.
If longer curved pieces are wanted before chaining, 22.5° is the parameter to
move — and moving it costs straight-edge discrimination everywhere else, which
is the argument for a curvature-aware predicate rather than a looser angle.

## A circle is not enough for this subject, but the evidence is thin

The prediction going in was that a nut's curves are circles seen obliquely, so
a circle primitive would be wrong and a conic needed. Axis ratios from the
conic fits back the first half: **1.13 to 3.85**, genuinely far from 1.

The second half did not survive contact:

**The conic wins 6 of 12, and the median improvement is 0.001 px.** On the
3.85-ratio chain it is worse than the circle. Four of twelve come back
*hyperbola*, which for a nut is nonsense.

Same cause for all of it: over 26–50° of sweep the extra parameters are not
identifiable, and an unconstrained algebraic fit wanders. That does not
overturn "the subject needs conics" — it fixes the **ordering**. Chain first,
fit the conic last on the longest span that can be assembled, and constrain to
ellipses when doing it. Fitting a conic per label, which was the first sketch,
is fitting noise.

## The overlay found the bug, again

First version of the chaining used near endpoints plus a small-but-nonzero
turn, with no fit test. The tables looked plausible — chains of 5 and 8 pieces,
sweeps of 134° and 327°. The overlay showed why: **it had chained its way
around the hexagon.** A polygon presents exactly that signature at every
vertex. The circle test that `merge` would call "the claim" is not optional,
and it refuses 17 of 45 candidates here.

The table would have said it too, in a column that run did not print: `circMax`
of 3.7 and 10.3 px against a 1.0 px tolerance. What it did print was `circRMS`,
and 1.4 px RMS did not look obviously wrong.

Second thing the picture showed and the table did not. The largest, smoothest,
best-conditioned chain in the frame (4 pieces, 77.5° of sweep, r = 54.9) has a
mean thinned magnitude of **0.015**, against **0.087** for the nut's actual top
rim — 5.8× weaker. It is one of the two **shadow blobs**. A soft shadow is
smooth and low-noise, which is to say it is the best-behaved arc in the image,
and an arc detector will love it.

Cannot call it properly: no AOVs ship with `assets/`, so `explain` cannot run,
and contrast is evidence rather than a verdict. But this is §11's shading
lesson queuing up to repeat for curves. **Regenerate the nut with `--truth
--aovs` and run `explain` over the chains before trusting any curve-recall
number.**

## `minMag` sweep

Chain count is not especially threshold-sensitive; segment count is.

```
minMag   segments   labels   arc-preferring   edge px   chains
 0.002        102       98                3      1944       23
 0.005         74       74                2      1616       23
 0.01          67       67                2      1493       20
 0.02          44       44                2      1038       15
```

Thinned magnitude on this image: max 0.101, mean 0.0011, stddev 0.0076.

## What this changes about the design sketch

1. **Chaining is the primary operation**, not a nicety. A per-label arc fit
   finds 2 of 74 and is close to useless alone.
2. **`angleTol`, not `maxResidual`**, is what fragments curves at these radii.
3. **Circles for the chaining decision** — closed form, only `+ - * / sqrt`, so
   §5-safe, and well conditioned on short spans. **Conic once, at the end,** on
   the assembled chain.
4. **Validate against `explain` before scoring anything**, because the
   strongest arcs in this image may be shadows.

## Still open

- Ground truth. `gt-edge` records are straight 2D segments off a tessellated
  mesh, so a curved silhouette arrives as a fan of chords and an arc has no
  single truth edge to match. `nearestAlong` already samples per pixel and
  takes a median, which transfers — but its *modal* vote does not: an arc
  crossing twelve chords would report one match out of twelve and read as
  catastrophic recall on a perfect detection. A coverage set, not a modal.
- Deeper than that: the silhouette of a smooth surface is where `n·v = 0`. It
  is not a mesh feature, does not lie on mesh edges, and moves with the camera.
  That is §11's T-junction problem, except on a curved subject it stops being
  occasional and becomes the majority case.
- The largest-gap construction for an arc's angular extent is written and not
  tested at every rotation. Min/max of the angle is wrong for any arc
  straddling the branch cut — it returns the two ends of the *gap*. Same class
  as the vector-sum-not-angle-average note in `k_segments`.
- The nut may be a poor scoring subject for the same reason the helmet is:
  `cv-lab-users-manual.md` records 6,672 truth edges, 2,548 visible, at 256 px.
  Arcs do not fix that, and the threads are most of that count.
