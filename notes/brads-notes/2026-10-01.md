# 2026-10-01

# The gap sweep's numbers moved, and the lab was not why

A small formatting change to the gap sweep's CSV was checked by re-running the
front-lit analysis with `--skip-render`. The numbers came out different from
the ones in [2026-09-30](2026-09-30.md):

| | 09-30 | today |
|---|---|---|
| 50 mm error | −0.304 px | −0.317 px |
| shading detections in the gap at 50 mm | 6 | 10 |
| 1 mm | no pair ("detected edges are not parallel") | pair found, +2.395 px |

The overlays showed the detected segments themselves had changed, not just the
analysis of them. Reproducibility is one of this project's two requirements,
so this was chased before anything else.

## The answer, first

**The renders had been replaced.** The front-lit sweep was run a second time
under the same `--name`, about 1.5 hours after the run whose numbers were
reported:

| file | written |
|---|---|
| the run log behind the 09-30 numbers | 09-30 17:22:54 |
| `generated/gap-1-front/shots.json` | 09-30 18:50:50 |
| the eight renders and their truth | 09-30 18:51–18:53 |

A gap-sweep run writes `shots.json` first and then renders, which matches
these times. Path tracing is not byte-reproducible: the lamp commit measured
two renders of one scene differing by 3.4–4.8 levels per pixel. Today's
`--skip-render` analysed a second sample of the same shots, and nothing in the
tooling said so.

## Ruled out on the way, with how

- **Inputs changed after they were written.** No file in the run was modified after the run that wrote it.
- **Code.** Nothing that produces detections changed between the runs; the only new file, `src/lab/gapsweep.js`, reads the output.
- **The addon.** There is one `cvlab.node`, built 09-20.
- **The display's colour profile reaching decoding.** `load` already passes `colorSpaceConversion: 'none'`.
- **The lab being nondeterministic.** The 50 mm image went through the lab three times with byte-identical `features.json`. All eight images were then run again, immediately after a GPU warm-up render of the same scene, and compared step by step with the session logs' output hashes: **all 19 steps of all 8 images identical.** The GPU guess was wrong.

**A mistake of mine worth recording:** I first said the renders were untouched
since they were written. I had compared the files' times with each other,
which only shows that they were all written together. The time that settled it
was the run log's. The question to ask is when the file was written relative
to the run that used it.

## What the accident measured

Two independent renders of the same shots, through the same lab, are a
direct measure of render noise. That settles one of 09-30's open questions:

| gap | run 1 error (px) | run 2 error (px) | run 1 spans both | run 2 spans both |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 mm | −0.304 | −0.317 | 0 | 0 |
| 20 mm | −0.403 | −0.371 | 0 | 0 |
| 10 mm | −0.161 | −0.168 | 0 | 0 |
| 5 mm | +0.021 | +0.016 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 mm | +1.190 | +1.206 | 0 | 0 |
| 1 mm | no pair | +2.395 | 1 | 1 |
| 0.5 mm | no pair | no pair | 2 | 1 |
| 0 mm | no pair | no pair | 0 | 0 |

- **20–50 mm:** the −0.3 to −0.4 px error repeats to within 0.03 px, so it is a
  bias, not noise.
- **2 mm:** the +1.2 px overestimate repeats to within 0.02 px.
- **1 mm:** whether there is a pair at all depends on the noise in that render.
  The answer there is "unstable", and a single run reporting either outcome is
  misleading.

Two samples is the minimum. The run-to-run spread at 1 mm needs more.

## What changed because of it

- **A run name is rendered once** (`7838868`). Rendering into a name whose
  `shots.json` exists is refused, saying when it was rendered. The three
  options are a new `--name`, `--skip-render`, or `--overwrite`.
- **Every analysis records what it measured** (`f729aea`). `gap-sweep.json`
  carries a SHA-256 of each shot's render, truth and three passes. A
  `--skip-render` over different files names the changed shots and keeps the
  previous record as `gap-sweep.replaced-<time>.json`.

  The first guard stops this happening through `gap-sweep`; the second catches
  it however it happens, for example `npm run generate` writing into the
  directory by hand.

The 09-30 records of `gap-1` and `gap-1-front` predate the hashes. They were
kept as `replaced-` files, with a note that they cannot be checked.

## Not settled

- **Whether the 20–50 mm bias belongs to the detector, the truth, or the
  measuring point.** The 09-30 sigma question applies here too, and the same
  sigma sweep over existing renders would answer both.
- **How unstable 1 mm is.** That needs several samples per gap: a
  `gap-1-front-s2`, `-s3`, and so on, now that names cannot collide.
- **A general version of the guard.** `npm run lab`'s session logs already hash
  every buffer they produce, including each `load`'s decoded image. Comparing
  two runs' `load` hashes would have shown the renders had changed at once.
  What lost that here was `--skip-render` writing its logs over the previous
  ones. Keeping or comparing them, as `gap-sweep.json` now does for its own
  record, is the general fix. It is not done.

# Later: the sigma test. Blur explains the small gaps and not the large ones

The 09-30 note guessed that the 2 mm overestimate was blur: at `sigma=1.4`, the
two gradient peaks of a 2.3 px strip are pushed outward. Earlier today, the
20–50 mm bias was left open as possibly the same thing. Both are questions
about the pipeline, answerable over the existing front-lit renders.
`gap-sweep` gained `--script` for this ("gap-sweep: --script runs another
pipeline over the same renders, into its own results").

There were four copies of `explained.lab`, differing only in
`gaussian(G, sigma=…)`, in `results/sigma-scripts/`, which is ignored. Each was
run with `npm run gap-sweep -- --name gap-1-front --scene saved:gap-1-front
--skip-render --script results/sigma-scripts/sigma-<σ>.lab`. The `sigma-1.4`
copy is byte-identical to `explained.lab` and served as the control: all eight
`features.json` matched the default run byte for byte.

## The answer, first

Error in px, measured − true, over the same eight renders:

| gap (true px) | σ=0.7 | σ=1.0 | σ=1.4 | σ=2.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 mm (59.7) | −0.350 | −0.316 | −0.317 | −0.315 |
| 20 mm (23.5) | −0.393 | −0.360 | −0.371 | −0.353 |
| 10 mm (11.7) | −0.255 | −0.238 | −0.168 | −0.035 |
| 5 mm (5.8) | −0.122 | −0.074 | +0.016 | +0.271 |
| 2 mm (2.3) | +0.234 | +0.470 | +1.206 | +2.242 |
| 1 mm (1.2) | cube edge lost | +1.773 | +2.395 | cube edge lost |
| 0.5 mm, 0 mm | lost | lost | lost | lost |

| | σ=0.7 | σ=1.0 | σ=1.4 | σ=2.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| segments, 50 mm / 2 mm | 226 / 171 | 193 / 195 | 135 / 141 | 86 / 90 |
| shading detections in the gap, 50 mm | 21 | 15 | 10 | 5 |

- **The small-gap overestimate is blur.** At 2 mm it grows almost tenfold
  from σ=0.7 to σ=2.0. At σ=2.0 it already shows at 5 mm, a gap of about 2.9σ.
  The 2 mm overlays show it directly: at σ=0.7 the two detected lines sit on
  their truth edges, and at σ=2.0 the cube's sits above its edge and the
  table's below.
- **The 20–50 mm bias is not blur.** It holds at −0.32 to −0.39 px across a
  threefold range of σ. A shift applied to the whole image would cancel in a
  gap, so this is something that moves one or both edges individually. Two
  untested guesses: a silhouette (the cube's bottom edge) and a crease (the
  table's top edge) placing their gradient peaks differently relative to the
  true geometric edge, or the antialiased boundary of a dark face against a
  lit one.
- **No σ resolves 1 mm.** At σ=0.7 noise breaks the cube's edge up; at
  σ=2.0 blur merges it into the table's. σ=1.0 finds a pair 1.8 px off a 1.2 px
  gap.
- **Small σ costs noise.** σ=0.7 has 2.6× the segments of σ=2.0 at 50 mm,
  and 4× the shading fragments in the gap. `corners` is quadratic in
  segment count.

For gaps of 2–5 mm at this scale (1.16 px per mm), σ≈0.7–1.0 cuts the error
2.5–5× compared with the default. Whether that is worth the noise depends on
what consumes the edges. Nothing here suggests changing the default yet. It
was chosen for whole images, not for two edges a few pixels apart.

## Not settled

- **The 20–50 mm bias.** Split the error per edge: compare each detected
  edge with its own truth edge, not only the gap between them. That needs
  no new renders.
- **σ chosen for the gap only.** A pipeline could blur less only where two
  edges are close. That is a design question, not a measurement.
- **One render set.** These numbers are from the second front-lit render
  set only. The first was overwritten (see above).
