# 2026-09-05

# What the helmet's segments are actually made of

The question §11 has been carrying since 08-27: the 156-segment count had no
breakdown, and everything needed to get one existed. Ran it. Six views, two
sizes, `--truth --aovs`, `pipelines/explained.lab` unchanged.

`--size 256` first, because **156 was measured at 256** and the default is 512.
Ran the default size before noticing, got 494 segments an image and briefly
thought the count had moved. It had not; the resolution had.

## The count reproduces

983 segments over six views at 256px — **164 an image against 156** on 08-27.
Same scene, same room, denoiser still off. Close enough to call it the same
measurement, and it is the first time that number has been reproduced at all.

## The breakdown, and it is not what the docs said

```
983 detected  ·  598 match real geometry (61%)  ·  385 invented

matched     crease 324 · silhouette 203 · boundary 71
invented    occlusion 282 (73%) · shading 46 (12%) · texture 32 (8%) · crease 25 (6%)
```

Four places in this repo say the helmet's image edges are "overwhelmingly
paint". **Texture is 8%.** At 512px it is 14%. It was never measured — it was
inferred from the model being a dense textured mesh, which is true and does not
imply it.

Nor is the detector mostly wrong on this subject: **61% of what it finds matches
real geometry**, which is a better precision than the cluttered cube scene
managed. The helmet is a fine fixture in the direction everyone assumed it was
bad at.

## The dominant invented bucket is occlusion, and that is the interesting part

282 of 385, and they are not threshold-grazing:

```
                       n     p10      median    p90
all detections       983   0.0082    0.0706    2.78
matched real         598   0.0100    0.0787    3.02
invented occlusion   282   0.0318    0.0884    0.178     (threshold 0.02 m)
```

The invented ones sit on **~9 cm depth steps, distributionally indistinguishable
from the ones that matched**. So they are on places where the geometry really
does step and ground truth does not pair them.

Two candidates and this run separates neither:

1. the matcher failing on a dense mesh — a fitted segment spans dozens of tiny
   truth edges and matches none of them cleanly;
2. grazing slant faking a step — `explain` samples 2.5px either side, and near
   the silhouette of a *curved* surface that lateral distance buys a large depth
   change with no occlusion anywhere. A cube's flat faces cannot do this.

The second is a defect in `explain` if it is real, and it would not have shown
up on any cube. Worth an experiment: sample offsets 1.5 / 2.5 / 4 px and see
whether the occlusion share tracks the offset. If it does, it is slant.

Either way it is the T-junction finding again — 37% there, **73% here**.

## Shading is confirmed by eye, which is the only part that needed no argument

The overlay makes two of them unambiguous: a red rectangle on the visor, which
is a specular highlight, and a red ring on the floor, which is the contact
shadow. Real image edges belonging to the light. `explain` called both shading
without being asked.

## §5's threshold does not survive this subject either

```
field          real range        invented range      best F1   clean
endpointGap    0.36 – 133.67     0.38 – 128.21         0.49     no
sigma          0.00 – 3.28       0.00 – 3.40           0.51     no
reach          0.27 – 136.28     0.54 – 131.39         0.47     no
```

Nothing clean, every range overlapping end to end, against 18.8x separation with
no overlap on the bare cube. The pattern from the clutter run holds and extends:
**the thresholds are a property of a bare cube, not of the detector.**

## A defect: ground truth's visibility depends on the render size

Same scene, same camera, same six shots:

| view | visible edges @256 | @512 |
|---|---|---|
| p0 | 3045 / 9572 | 2587 / 9572 |
| p1 | 3531 / 9531 | 2904 / 9531 |
| p2 | 3569 / 9530 | 2906 / 9530 |

**18% more edges are called visible at the lower resolution.** The visibility
test rasterises at render size, so a coarser depth buffer occludes less of a
dense mesh. The total edge count is identical, as it must be — only the
visible fraction moves.

That is why recall reads 4% at 256 and 12% at 512. The denominator moved. Any
recall number compared across sizes is comparing two different truth sets, and
nothing in the tally says so.

Not obviously wrong — visibility at the resolution you rendered is a defensible
definition — but it is undocumented, and it silently makes recall
resolution-dependent in the direction that flatters the bigger image.

## Caveats

- Denoiser off, as everything on record has been. The scattered isolated
  detections on the flat wall in the overlay are the path-traced noise floor.
- One scene, one detector configuration, `explain` at its default thresholds.
- 512px numbers are in the same directories if wanted: precision 45%, recall
  12%, invented segments occlusion 59% / shading 22% / texture 14% / crease 5%.
  The shift toward shading and texture with resolution is real and unexplained.
- Corners at 256: 2640 detected, 799 real, 30% precision. Invented corners are
  occlusion 1335 (73%), shading 274 (15%), crease 119 (6%), texture 113 (6%) —
  the same shape as the segments, which is expected since a corner is made of
  two of them.
