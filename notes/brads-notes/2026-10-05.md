## The pose, carried

Follows `2026-10-04.md` (carrying a gap, then the trackPair op).

Brad: carry the previous frame's pose into the position solve.

solvePosition takes a prior (mean, covariance). `npm run position --
--sequence <dir>` solves a sweep frame by frame, alone and carried: prior =
last solution + commanded move, covariance + motion^2.

**First run was wrong.** Prior started at the TRUE pose, moves were the TRUE
moves. One view's unseen direction just kept the truth: everything 0.01-0.05
mm, single views included. Redone with the commanded poses = truth + random
error (start 1 mm, 0.1 mm per move, 20 seeded trials) and a dead-reckoning
line to beat (0.7/1.1/0.9 mm).

## Is the light the next gain?

Brad asked whether moving the light was the most likely next gain. It was a
guess. First check, from data already made: on the test poses explain()
labels pair 2's detected edges crease in 29 of 33 rows, not shading -- and
those rows still have 0.165 px RMS refit error (pair 1, same labels: 0.032).
So not the stack-1 failure (the edge being a shadow boundary) again, though
a shading gradient at the crease would still be labelled crease.

Then the test: the x sweep (pair 2 sees x) under two more lights, the same
distance (~1.1 m) from the cubes. scenes/stack-3.json swings the light round
toward the side face; stack-4.json raises it over the front.

    refit error px     pair 1 rms  pair 2 rms  pair 2 within-view sd  pair 2 read
    stack-2 (as was)   0.029       0.172       0.134                  19/26
    stack-3 (side)     0.044       0.217       0.073                  19/26
    stack-4 (high)     1.339       1.397       1.105                  18/26

- Side light: pair 2's scatter halves, but it reads ~0.2 px short in every
  view. The picture shows why: that gap's strip, lit from its own side, is
  hardly darker than the faces. Scatter traded for bias.
- High light: the base's top face is lit like its front face, the crease
  vanishes, and pair 1 reads the shadow under the top cube instead: 3.1 px
  for 4.8, every frame. The eighth's failure, on the other pair.
- So the light is a lever, and stack-2's is the best of the three. Neither
  new one makes pair 2 better overall.

What the side light suggests: a bias that holds still is removable, scatter
is not. The solve's reference readings come from the truth; taken instead
from the readings at a known pose -- a calibration, the robot placing the
part where it says -- a constant bias would cancel. Under the side light
that leaves pair 2's 0.073, against 0.134 under stack-2's. Untested.
