# 2026-10-02

# The gap is in the pixels. The pipeline is what loses it

10-01 closed with one thing outweighing the rest: at 2 mm the gap reads 1.2 px
too wide, and at 1 mm and below there is no reading at all. The sigma test
showed that was blur and that no σ fixes it: σ = 0.7 gets 2 mm to +0.23 px and
loses the cube's edge at 1 mm to noise.

Before changing the pipeline, one question had to be answered, because the
answer bounds everything after it: **is the gap in the render at all at 2, 1
and 0.5 mm, or has the pixel grid already averaged it away?** If it is not
there, no pipeline finds it and the floor is about 2 mm at this scale.

The script and how to rerun it are in
[`2026-10-02-gap-profile/`](2026-10-02-gap-profile/README.md).

## The answer, first

**It is there, down to 0.5 mm (0.58 px).** A model of two steps fitted to the
unblurred pixels reads it to within 0.1–0.2 px, on both linear render sets.

Gap error in px, measured − true; the two numbers are the two independent
renders (`gap-1-front-linear`, `-linear-2`):

| gap (true px) | pipeline, σ = 1.4 | two-step fit, everything free | two-step fit, strip level carried |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 mm (5.82) | +0.10 / +0.10 | −0.07 / −0.06 | (the source of the level) |
| 2 mm (2.32) | +1.18 / +1.22 | −0.14 / −0.12 | −0.09 / −0.09 |
| 1 mm (1.16) | no pair | −0.41 / −0.37 | −0.15 / −0.14 |
| 0.5 mm (0.58) | no pair | +0.36 / +0.12 | −0.19 / −0.19 |

Read as gaps, the last column is 2.23, 1.01 and 0.39 px for 2.32, 1.16 and
0.58. It is monotone, and the two renders agree to 0.01 px.

## What was measured

Every pixel in a band around the truth pair is one sample, at its own signed
distance from the Table's truth edge. No blur and no interpolation. The edge
runs about 8° off the pixel grid, so 105 px of it covers every sub-pixel
phase, the way a slanted-edge test does.

The raw profile at 1 mm, in 0.25 px bins (`h` is px from the Table's truth
edge, toward the Cube; the Cube's truth edge is at +1.16):

```
h= -1.125  0.6097   table front
h= -0.375  0.5015
h= +0.125  0.3090
h= +0.625  0.1849   the strip: table top in the cube's shadow
h= +0.875  0.1865
h= +1.375  0.2874
h= +1.875  0.3034   cube face
```

Two steps with a dip between them, in a gap narrower than 1.2 px. At 0.5 mm
the dip is still there, 0.289 against a face of 0.305, and nothing else is.

The model: three plateaus (table front T, strip S, cube face F), two step
positions, each step averaged over the pixel's own footprint, plus a small
Gaussian blur. Fitted to the samples directly, 1300–7700 of them per frame.

## The three findings

**1. At 2 mm the strip reaches its plateau, so everything can be fitted
free.** +1.18 / +1.22 px becomes −0.14 / −0.12. The fitted strip level is
0.114 / 0.118, against 0.12 at 5, 10 and 20 mm.

**2. Below about 1.5 px, width and darkness trade off.** A pixel averages
what it covers, so a narrow dark strip and a wider, less dark one put the same
light on the sensor. Holding the gap error at a value and refitting everything
else (run 1; rms of the fit, and the strip level it needed):

| gap error held | −0.6 | −0.3 | −0.15 | 0 | +0.15 | +0.3 | +0.6 | +1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 mm, rms | .0344 | .0299 | .0289 | .0289 | .0298 | .0314 | .0359 | .0424 |
| 2 mm, rms | .0231 | .0184 | .0176 | .0181 | .0197 | .0221 | .0276 | .0343 |
| 1 mm, rms | .0302 | .0302 | .0303 | .0306 | .0311 | .0317 | .0332 | .0353 |
| 1 mm, S | −0.095 | 0.065 | 0.108 | 0.138 | 0.161 | 0.179 | 0.205 | 0.229 |
| 0.5 mm, rms | — | .0315 | .0315 | .0315 | .0314 | .0314 | .0315 | .0315 |
| 0.5 mm, S | — | 0.156 | 0.223 | 0.250 | 0.264 | 0.273 | 0.282 | 0.289 |

At 5 and 2 mm the rms has a minimum. At 1 mm it is flat from −0.6 to 0, and the
free fit slides down it to a strip of 0.02–0.04, a third of what the strip is
at every larger gap. At 0.5 mm it is flat everywhere: any width fits, given the
level to go with it. What the pixels hold there is the product, not the width.

**3. Carrying the strip level in removes the trade-off.** With S and the blur
taken from the same run's 5 mm fit (0.123; 0.29 and 0.27), the fit has one
answer at every gap, the last column of the first table. This is not an
arbitrary prior. S is observable while the gap is wide, it was steady at 0.12
from 20 mm to 2 mm in both runs, and an approach passes through the wide gaps
first.

## What else it showed

- **Why the blur's push-apart fell on the cube's edge** (+1.14 against −0.04,
  10-01). The Table's step is 0.60 → 0.12 and the Cube's is 0.12 → 0.31, less
  than half as tall. Two opposite steps a few pixels apart, blurred: the
  larger one's gradient tail displaces the smaller one's peak. Not tested
  beyond the arithmetic agreeing in sign and side.
- **What is left is on the cube's edge, toward the gap.** With the level
  carried, the Table's edge is within ±0.03 px and the Cube's is −0.06, −0.15
  and −0.20 px at 2, 1 and 0.5 mm. That is the sign and the size of the
  cube-edge offset closed unexplained on 10-01 (`design-lab-model.md` §5).
  So this may be the fit reading the render faithfully. It grows as the gap
  narrows, which 10-01's offset did not obviously do, so it may also be the
  assumed S being slightly high. One number cannot separate them.

## A mistake on the way

The first run of the free fit at 1 mm returned −0.41 px with S = 0.024, and at
0.5 mm +0.36 px in one render and +0.12 in the other. I read that as the method
failing below 2 mm. It was the model having one more freedom than the pixels
constrain. The held-gap scan showed it: an rms that does not move is not a bad
fit, it is an unanswerable question. Asking the question with S supplied gave
the same answer twice.

## What this does not show

- **It is seeded from ground truth.** The band, the starting positions, and
  the edges' convergence over the window (2.25 → 2.40 px at 2 mm) all come from
  the truth pair. A real operation starts from detections, and at 1 mm and
  below the pipeline detects no cube edge to start from.
- **One edge, well placed.** 105 px long, 8° off the grid, flat plateaus on
  both sides, and a strip darker than both. A short edge, an axis-aligned one,
  or a strip between its neighbours in brightness is untested.
- **S below 2 mm is assumed.** Nothing in the image says the strip keeps its
  level as the gap closes.
- **One discrepancy with 10-01, unexplained.** At 20 mm this fit puts the
  cube's edge at −0.07 / −0.08 px. The pipeline put it at −0.21 / −0.23, and
  the design doc says the render's own step sits where the detector puts it to
  ~0.05 px. At 50 mm they do agree (−0.27 / −0.23 here, −0.29 / −0.23 there).
  The fits differ in what they assume beside the edge: flat plateaus within
  6 px here, a 50% crossing there.
- **The fit's rms differs between renders of one shot** by up to 2×: 0.0176
  and 0.0345 at 2 mm, 0.0302 and 0.0179 at 1 mm. The gap readings agree to
  0.01 px regardless. Probably a few bright samples in an undenoised
  96-sample render. Not looked at.

# Later: `fitPairs`, from detections. And the aperture, which was wrong twice

The section above was seeded from ground truth. This is the operation: it
starts from the segments `fit` reports and nothing else.
`P = fitPairs(F, G)`, where `G` is the gray image before `gaussian`. For every
two segments within 6 px, 5° and 10 px of overlap, it fits three plateaus and
two straight steps to the unblurred pixels around them (`src/lab/pairs.js`).
`pipelines/pairs.lab` is `explained.lab` plus that line, and `gap-sweep` reads
`P` when a script binds it.

## The answer, first

Gap error in px, both linear renders, `--script pipelines/pairs.lab`:

| gap (true px) | detected | refit | refit: cube edge / table edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 mm (5.82) | +0.103, +0.098 | −0.074, −0.055 | −0.033 / +0.041, −0.060 / −0.004 |
| 2 mm (2.32) | +1.179, +1.224 | −0.098, −0.049 | −0.064 / +0.034, −0.027 / +0.022 |
| 1 mm and below | no pair | no pair to refit | |

**It does not matter which blur found the edges.** The same script at three
values of σ, 2 mm:

| σ | detected, run 1 / run 2 | refit, run 1 / run 2 |
|---|---|---|
| 1.4 | +1.179 / +1.224 | −0.098 / −0.049 |
| 1.0 | +0.552 / +0.613 | −0.077 / −0.049 |
| 0.7 | +0.363 / +0.385 | −0.071 / −0.033 |

Over the other scenes' existing results (run through the same module, not
through the operation): `gap-1-front-matte` 2 mm +0.07; `gap-1-front-low` 5 mm
−0.14, 2 mm −0.13, and **1 mm +0.07**, where its detector did find both edges
(3.14 px detected, 1.18 refit, 1.16 true). ACES `gap-1-front`: 2 mm −0.28, of
which +0.28 is the table edge where the tone curve put it, and 1 mm +0.04.

About 50–90 ms per image, nearly all of it measuring the aperture.

## Dead ends, with numbers

**1. The aperture assumed.** The first version took a pixel to average exactly
its own square. At 2 mm it barely matters (−0.07 at an aperture of 1, −0.10 at
1.25, −0.15 at 1.5). Seeded from truth at 1 mm it decides the answer: +0.15 px
at 1, −0.26 at 1.4, with a `gapSigma` of 0.04 both times. A confident number
that moves 0.4 px with an assumption is not a measurement.

**2. The aperture fitted per pair.** That fixed 1 mm and broke something
worse. A test I had written for a different reason, one step detected twice
1.5 px apart, came back as a pair 0.497 px apart with a strip exactly midway
between the two levels, an aperture of 0.506, and a `gapSigma` of 0.0017. It
is an exact identity, not a bad fit: a step through an aperture of 1 is two
steps half a pixel apart through an aperture of a half. With the aperture
free, any soft edge is a narrow gap.

**What it is now.** The aperture is the camera's. It is measured on the
image's lone segments, each fitted as one step, and held for the pairs. On
`gap-2mm` (run 1) the fifteen lone segments over 20 px returned 0.82 to 1.71
for geometry and 5.2 to 7.8 for the three shadow edges in the 50 mm frame.
The lower quartile is taken, 1.09 to 1.28 depending on the frame. With that,
seeded at 1 mm, strip level fitted: 0.967 and 0.965 px in the two renders
(true 1.161), with fitted strip levels of 0.123 and 0.121 against the 0.123
the 5 mm frame gives. So **at 1.16 px the strip's level does not need carrying
in**, which the section above said it did. That conclusion was drawn with the
blur fitted alongside. At 0.58 px it still does: nothing in one render,
0.91 ± 0.13 in the other; held, 0.25 and 0.28.

**3. A fixture with one row of plateau.** Three axis-aligned edges 3 px apart,
`pad=2`, detections 1 px off. The fit returned an exact solution that was not
the drawn one: edge 0 at −1.87 with a level of −2.52 beyond it. One row of
pixels on that side cannot tell a level from a position. Not a bug in the fit.
It is what `pad` is for, and the default of 4 is not generous.

**4. An edge exactly on a pixel boundary had no gradient.** `density`
returned 0 at |d| = a + b, and an axis-aligned edge sitting on a boundary has
both neighbouring rows exactly there. The fit could not move. `>` instead of
`>=`.

**5. Found by typing it into the app.** `X = fitPairs(F, A)` with a colour `A`
ran, fitted the red channel, and reported a pair. The registry declares
`channels: [1]` and nothing enforces it for a JavaScript kernel. The kernel
refuses now, and `test/kernels.js` holds it to that.

## What `gapSigma` is worth

It is the fit's curvature under its own residual. At 2 mm it says 0.015 and
0.027; the two renders differ by 0.049, and both are 0.05–0.10 from the truth.
So it orders fits correctly (0.58 px free: 0.13; 2.3 px: 0.015) and understates
the error. Some of what it misses is not noise at all: the cube edge's offset
that 10-01 closed unexplained is in every one of these readings.

## Not settled

- **1 mm and below.** The pipeline finds one edge there, so there is no pair.
  The fit handles the pixels (seeded: −0.19 at 1.16 px). What is missing is
  a way to ask it: test a single segment for a strip hidden inside it.
- **Carrying the strip level** from a wide frame to a narrow one, for 0.58 px.
  `strip=held, stripLevel=` exists; nothing fills it in.
- **Lower quartile, or median?** The quartile gave 1.09–1.28 across frames of
  one camera, which is a wide spread for a constant. The two 500 px edges
  alone say 1.17–1.31.
- **A third edge in the band.** At 10 mm the cube's edge is paired with the
  shadow boundary 3.2 px from it, and fitted as if that were a hard step.

# Last: the strip hidden inside one segment. Found at 1 mm; not read well

At 1 mm (1.16 px) the linear renders have no pair for `fitPairs` to place. The
blur has merged the two edges, and what the detector reports is one 512 px
segment along the Table's front edge. The strip under the Cube is hidden in
114 px of it.

`H = findPairs(F, G)` walks every segment that is in no pair, 24 px at a time
with half overlapping, and asks the unblurred pixels whether each window is
one step or two. Runs of windows that say two are joined and fitted once more.
The records are `edge-pair` with both edges naming the same segment.
`pipelines/pairs.lab` now runs both operations, and `gap-sweep` reads either.

## The answer, first

Gap error in px, render 1 / render 2, from the detections alone:

| gap (true px) | detected | refit | from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 mm (5.82) | +0.10 / +0.10 | −0.07 / −0.04 | `fitPairs` |
| 2 mm (2.32) | +1.18 / +1.22 | −0.11 / −0.08 | `fitPairs` |
| 1 mm (1.16) | no pair | **−0.25 / −0.28** | `findPairs` |
| 0.5 mm, contact | no pair | nothing | |

The 1 mm row at σ = 1.0 and 0.7: −0.25 / −0.28, −0.24 / −0.28. With the strip
level held at 0.124: −0.16 / −0.14. `gap-1-front-matte` at 1 mm: −0.21.

**It finds the strip and where it is.** The gain, two steps against one on the

| frame | windows under the Cube | windows clear of it |

| 1 mm, render 1 | 1.35 to 1.63 | 1.00 to 1.04 |

Over the 48 frames of six sweeps it reported a hidden pair in six: the 1 mm gap
three times (both linear renders and matte), a strip under the Cube's side
face at 2 mm twice (below), and one misread.

## Two corrections to this morning

**1. The aperture is the median now, not the lower quartile.** A test fixture
with two lone segments returned 0.93 for an image drawn through 1.4. The lower
quartile of two values is the smaller, and the smaller was the segment hiding
the strip: a step with a dark strip against it fits sharper than the image
is. So "nothing reads sharper than the camera", which was the whole argument
for the quartile, is false. Measured over the sixteen frames of the two
sweeps:

| | range | sd |
|---|---|---|
| lower quartile | 1.06 to 1.31 | 0.081 |
| median | 1.31 to 1.39 | 0.022 |
| median weighted by length × step² / rms² | 1.27 to 1.40 | 0.032 |

That answers the question the section above left open. `fitPairs` is v2. And
`findPairs` leaves the segment it is searching out of its own aperture.

**2. "At 1.16 px the strip's level does not need carrying in" was wrong.** It
was read off 0.967 and 0.965 with fitted levels of 0.123 and 0.121. Those came
from apertures of 1.26 and 1.21, which is where the quartile happened to land
in those two frames. With the median (1.34, 1.37), `02-seeded.js` gives:

| true gap | level fitted | level held at 0.123 |
|---|---|---|
| 2.32 px | 2.218, 2.248 | 2.239, 2.229 |
| 1.16 px | 0.918, 0.864 (levels 0.102, 0.076) | 0.993, 1.020 |
| 0.58 px | 1.019, 0.990 (± 0.17, 0.13) | 0.374, 0.400 |

The reading at 1.16 px moves 0.07 px for 0.08 of aperture. Two renders
agreeing with each other was not evidence that either was right; they shared
the aperture. The first section's conclusion stands after all: under about a
pixel and a half, the level has to be supplied.

## Dead ends, with numbers

**The first two-step fits under the Cube returned nothing at all.** Started
with the second edge 1 px out and levels from region means, the fit closed the
strip up instead of finding it. Three starting widths (reach/4, /2, reach)
with the best kept: 0.7 to 1.05 px in every window.

**The gain was compared across different pixels.** A one-step fit on its own
band against a two-step fit on a wider one gave "gains" of 1.05 to 1.10
everywhere. On the same pixels, 1.00 to 1.03.

**Second edges 4 to 15 px away.** With no bound, the second edge left the band
and settled on whatever was out there: gaps of 4.3, 6.8, 14.6, 15.3 px at gains
of 1.3 to 3.0. `gap <= reach`.

**The run overshot the strip.** In a fixture with a strip over 60 px of a
segment, the stretch came back as 43..115 for a strip at 50..110, because a
window half on the strip passes when the data are clean. Runs of three windows
or more now give up half a window at each end.

**A bright strip half a pixel wide on the Cube's left edge**, 0.9° off
vertical, in one render of two, at a gain of 1.40. Along a near-axis-aligned
edge every pixel crosses at the same phase, which is the reason such a segment
says nothing about the aperture either. A window that climbs less than one
pixel is not searched.

**The gain gate at 1.2 let in a misread**: `gap-1-front-low` at 0.5 mm, read
as 1.01 px at 1.22. Raised to 1.3. After the trimming and the median, the
same frame passes again at 1.31 and 1.35 and reads 1.02. So the gate is not
what keeps that out, and nothing is.

## Cost

About a second an image, against 0.06 s for the rest of the pipeline. Most
windows are a no, and a second edge with nothing to find wanders until the
iterations run out. Capped at 40 for the windows: 2.5 times faster, and the
same records on every frame tried. Uncapped it was 2.1 to 3.5 s.

## Not settled

- **Why the fitted strip comes out too dark and too narrow at 1.16 px.** The
  model has flat plateaus and a square aperture. The strip is a shadowed
  surface seen at a grazing angle, and may not be flat.
- **Carrying the level.** Still nothing fills `stripLevel` in. The held rows
  above were typed by hand from the 5 mm fit.
- **0.5 mm against contact.** With the level held they separate (0.35 against
  nothing). Without it they do not.
- **The gain threshold** rests on two renders of one scene.
- **`gap-1-front-matte` at 5 mm has no refit**: its detections are 6.07 px
  apart and `maxGap` is 6.

# After that: 2048 px. The strip keeps its level, and it is not flat

Carrying the strip's level from a wide frame to a narrow one assumes the level
does not change as the gap closes. At 512 px nothing can check that below
2 mm, because no pixel is wholly inside the strip. So the same shots at four
times the resolution, where 1 mm is 4.6 px and 0.5 mm is 2.3:

```bash
npm run gap-sweep -- --name gap-1-front-2048 --scene saved:gap-1-front --size 2048 \
                     --gaps 5,2,1,0.5,0 --script pipelines/pairs.lab
node notes/brads-notes/2026-10-02-gap-profile/04-strip-level.js gap-1-front-2048 pairs
```

About 4 minutes a render. One render set, so nothing here has a second sample.

## The answer, first

**The level holds to 1 mm and is a little darker at 0.5 mm.** Read straight
off the pixels at least 1 px inside both edges, no model:

| gap | width at 2048 px | strip, core mean | level `fitPairs` fitted |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 mm | 23.3 px | 0.119 | (no pair: over `maxGap`) |
| 2 mm | 9.3 px | 0.122 | (no pair: over `maxGap`) |
| 1 mm | 4.6 px | 0.116 | 0.112 |
| 0.5 mm | 2.3 px | 0.104 | 0.102 |

Within 5% down to 1 mm. About 13% darker at 0.5 mm, though that core is the
middle 0.3 px of the strip and 126 pixels. Held at 0.124 where the truth is
0.104, a 0.58 px gap at 512 px would read about 10% wide, +0.06 px. That is
small beside the −0.22 it reads short, and the other way. **So carrying the
level is sound.**

## What it showed that was not asked

| contact | no reading | no reading |

apart. The 0.5 mm row is the 512 px sweep's 2 mm row over again, in pixels:

−0.11 and −0.08). The problem was always in pixels. Everything
`findPairs` struggles with at 512 px is a regime that four times the
resolution moves out of.
